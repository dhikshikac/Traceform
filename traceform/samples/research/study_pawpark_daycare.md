# Research study: Paw Park Dog Daycare website

Fictional study for testing Traceform. All names, quotes and numbers are made up.

- **Client:** Paw Park, a dog daycare and boarding business in Ithaca, NY (opened 2021, 2 locations)
- **Researcher:** Harry Lin
- **Dates:** Oct 20 – Nov 3, 2026
- **Methods:** 2 staff interviews, 5 customer interviews, 1 online survey (n=112), 3 hours of observation at drop-off
- **Goal:** understand what dog owners need from the website before redesigning it. The current site is a single page with a phone number and a PDF price list.

---

## 1. Interview: Rosa Delgado, owner

*Oct 20, 2026, 45 minutes, in the office at the Elmira Road location*

**Q: Why redo the website now?**
Rosa: "The phone rings all morning and it's always the same three questions: is there space tomorrow, how much is it, and can my dog come if he's not neutered yet. My staff should be with the dogs, not on the phone."

**Q: How do people book today?**
Rosa: "They call or they text my personal number. I keep the bookings in a paper calendar and a Google Sheet, and the two never match. Last month we double-booked a boarding room over Thanksgiving weekend."

**Q: What makes Paw Park different?**
Rosa: "We cap it at 30 dogs per location. Most big daycares put 80 dogs in a warehouse. We split the dogs by size and energy, not just by size. A shy lab doesn't go in with the wrestlers."

**Q: What should the website feel like?**
Rosa: "Calm. Honest. Every dog daycare site is cartoon paw prints, bright orange and a stock photo of a golden retriever jumping through a sprinkler. Our dogs are mostly napping by 2 p.m. I want people to see that, because a tired, calm dog is what they're paying for."

**Q: Anything you never want on the site?**
Rosa: "No paw print bullet points. No 'fur baby'. And please don't promise 'cage-free' in big letters. We use crates for nap time on purpose, and I'd rather explain why than hide it."

**Q: What do new customers worry about?**
Rosa: "Fights and injuries, mostly. And whether their dog will be stressed. Every new dog does a half-day trial first, and I wish more people knew that before they called."

## 2. Interview: Jamal Price, lead handler

*Oct 21, 2026, 25 minutes, in the play yard during nap time*

**Q: What do owners ask you at pickup?**
Jamal: "'Did he eat?' 'Did she play with anyone?' 'Was he good?' They want a little report card. Right now I just tell them out loud, and half of it they forget by the time they get to the car."

**Q: What about photos?**
Jamal: "We post photos on Instagram, but owners always ask for photos of their dog specifically. I take maybe 40 photos a day on my own phone and text them to people. It takes forever."

**Q: Anything that surprises new customers?**
Jamal: "The vaccine rules. You need rabies, distemper and bordetella, and a lot of people find out at the front desk and have to go home. That's the worst way to start."

**Q: How do the dogs spend the day?**
Jamal: "Play in the morning, lunch, then a two-hour nap from 12 to 2, then a calmer afternoon. People think daycare is nonstop play. It isn't, and it shouldn't be."

## 3. Customer interviews

*Oct 24 – Oct 30, 2026, 10–20 minutes each, by phone or at pickup*

### P1: Megan, nurse, works 12-hour shifts, 1 dog (border collie mix)
- Uses daycare 3 days a week and books around her shift schedule.
- "I book at 11 p.m. after a shift. If I have to call during business hours, it's not happening."
- Wants to see availability for the next two weeks at once.

### P2: Tom and Erin, new parents, 1 dog (senior beagle)
- First time using any daycare; the dog is 11 years old.
- "We were scared he'd get knocked over by young dogs."
- Chose Paw Park because a friend mentioned the size and energy groups. Didn't find that on the website.

### P3: Priya, grad student, 1 dog (rescue pit mix)
- Price-sensitive and compares daycares on cost first.
- "I just want to see the price without downloading a PDF on my phone."
- Worried her dog will be judged by breed. "Some places turn you away the second you say pit bull."

### P4: Dan, works from home, 2 dogs (labs)
- Uses boarding more than daycare, mostly around holidays.
- "Holidays fill up in September. I found that out the hard way."
- Would pay a deposit to hold a room.

### P5: Linda, retired, 1 dog (anxious rescue greyhound)
- Prefers to call and talk to a person.
- "I don't want an app. I want to talk to Rosa."
- Values the nap-time crates: "My dog needs a quiet place, I'm glad they have one."

## 5. Observation notes: morning drop-off

*Oct 28, 2026, 7:00–10:00 a.m., front desk at the Elmira Road location*

- 23 dogs dropped off in 3 hours. The busiest window was 7:30–8:15 a.m.
- The phone rang 14 times during drop-off; 9 of the calls were about availability.
- 2 owners were turned away because of missing vaccine records.
- One owner asked to see the play room before leaving; staff walked her back, which took about 5 minutes.
- The front desk has a printed "today's groups" board (Small & Gentle, Medium Mixed, Big & Busy). Several owners stopped to read it.

---

## 6. Researcher's notes: open questions and tensions

- **Online vs. phone:** 71% would book online, but long-time customers like Linda want to talk to a person. Online booking can't replace the phone entirely.
- **"Cage-free":** competitors advertise it. Rosa refuses to, because nap crates are deliberate. The site has to explain the crates, not hide them.
- **Price first vs. safety first:** survey answers are close (37% price, 34% safety). Both need to be visible without scrolling.
- **Breed concerns:** P3 worries about breed bans. Rosa says Paw Park does not ban breeds and assesses each dog in the trial day. This isn't stated anywhere today.
- **Still unknown:** exact prices for 2027, staff-to-dog ratio (Rosa said "about 1 to 10" but wasn't sure), and whether the second location (Triphammer Road) has the same nap setup.
